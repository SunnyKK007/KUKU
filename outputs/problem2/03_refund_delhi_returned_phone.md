### What the law says (only verified sources)
- **You are a 'consumer', even for online purchases.** If you paid for goods or a service for your own use (not for resale or business), you are a 'consumer'. This includes things bought online. (Section 2(7) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-CONSUMER]
- **Not giving the service you paid for is a 'deficiency'.** Any fault, shortcoming or inadequacy in a service you paid for, including negligence or hiding relevant information from you, is a 'deficiency in service'. You can complain about it. (Section 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Section 2(6) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-DEFICIENCY]
- **Online platforms must answer your complaint within one month.** Every e-commerce platform, and every seller on a marketplace, must have a grievance officer whose contact details are shown on the platform. The officer must acknowledge your complaint within 48 hours and resolve it within one month. Accepted refunds must be paid within a reasonable time. (Rule 4(4) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020; Rule 4(5) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020; Rule 4(10) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020; Rule 6(4) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020) [ECOM-GRIEVANCE]
- **National Consumer Helpline: 1915.** The government's National Consumer Helpline is free. Call 1915 (8 AM to 8 PM), or WhatsApp/SMS 8800001915, or use consumerhelpline.gov.in, the NCH app or UMANG. It works in 17 languages and tries to settle the complaint with the company before you go to a Commission. [NCH]
- **The Commission can order a refund with interest, plus compensation.** If your complaint is proved, the Consumer Commission can order the seller to return the price you paid with interest, and to pay compensation for loss caused by their negligence. (Section 39(1)(c) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Section 39(1)(d) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-RELIEF]
- **You can file where you live, and you can file online.** You can file a consumer complaint in the District Commission where you live or work, or where the seller does business, or where the problem arose. The complaint can be filed electronically. (Section 34(2) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Section 35(1) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-WHERE-FILE]
- **District Commission handles claims up to Rs 50 lakh.** Since 30 Dec 2021, the District Consumer Commission hears complaints where what you paid is up to Rs 50 lakh. Above Rs 50 lakh up to Rs 2 crore goes to the State Commission; above Rs 2 crore to the National Commission. (the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021; Section 34(1) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-JURISDICTION]
- **No filing fee for claims up to Rs 5 lakh.** There is no fee to file a consumer complaint where what you paid is up to Rs 5 lakh. Above Rs 5 lakh up to Rs 10 lakh the fee is Rs 200. (Rule 7 of the Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020) [CPA-FEE]
- **File a consumer complaint within 2 years.** A consumer complaint must be filed within two years from when the problem arose. A late complaint is accepted only if you show 'sufficient cause' and the Commission records its reasons. (Section 69 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [CPA-LIMITATION]
- **File consumer cases online on e-Jagriti.** Consumer complaints to District, State and National Commissions are filed online on e-Jagriti (e-jagriti.gov.in). It replaced the older e-Daakhil portal from 1 Jan 2025 and supports OTP registration, online fee payment and virtual hearings. [EJAGRITI]
- **You may be entitled to a free lawyer.** Free legal services are available to, among others: women and children, members of Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes, persons with disability, industrial workmen, people in custody, victims of trafficking or disasters, and people below the income limit set by the state.
