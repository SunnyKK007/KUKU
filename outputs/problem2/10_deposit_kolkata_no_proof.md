### What the law says (only verified sources)
- **Rent and deposit rules are set by each state.** There is no single national law on security deposits. Tenancies are governed by each state's own rental law, plus what your rent agreement says. - **Breaking an agreement makes the other side liable to compensate you.** When someone breaks an agreement (like a rent agreement that says the deposit is refundable, or a job offer that fixes your salary), the person who suffers can claim compensation for the loss that naturally follows from the breach. ### How strong is your proof: **weak**
Missing or not confirmed, and how to fix it:
- **Rent agreement.** No agreement? Old rent receipts, bank transfers of monthly rent, or messages with the landlord about rent also show there was a tenancy.
- **Proof you paid the deposit (bank transfer, UPI, cheque or receipt).** Ask your bank for a statement showing the transfer. If you paid cash, look for any message where the landlord mentions the deposit amount, or a witness who was present.
- **Photos/video or a handover note from move-out day.** If you have no move-out photos, a message sent on the day you returned the keys helps. Ask anyone who was there to confirm the flat's condition.

4. **Civil recovery.** If they still do not pay, the next step is a money recovery case in the civil court for breach of the agreement. A lawyer (or free legal aid) should handle this step. [CONTRACT-BREACH]
